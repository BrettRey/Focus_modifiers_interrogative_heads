## Why

An October 2026 run (corpus searches, two independent audits, judgment
checks) changed what the study needs to test.

- **Headed relatives behave as predicted.** In COCA, *N exactly who*,
  *N who else*, *N who the hell* and P *exactly which* have zero relative
  tokens, where the interrogative rate in the same frames predicts roughly
  190 to 3,800 each. Usage evidence, not judgments.
- ***-ever* free relatives don't.** Post-head *exactly/precisely* is
  attested in argument position (*X11 will still support whatever exactly
  this feature is*), and *whoever/whatever the hell* is common, including
  edited text. CGEL p. 989's star on \**Whoever the hell said that was
  wrong* is wrong; Tredinnick (1994, via Dayal 1997, pp. 102, 106) already
  had *-ever* free relatives licensing *the hell* and plain ones not.
- **A rating contrast.** In five attested frames, plain *what* + *exactly*
  is worse than *whatever* + *exactly*, while plain *what* without
  *exactly* is fine (Brett's judgments). Brett is running that as a small
  rating study of his own (`../study-ever-exactly/protocol.md`,
  sixteen frames, *whatever/what* × ±*exactly*).

Two accounts compete for that contrast. On the *backgrounding* account,
*exactly*'s acceptability tracks how backgrounded the relative clause's
content is, so the *whatever* and *what* frames should differ only if
their content differs in backgroundedness. On the *open-identity*
account, *exactly* needs the referent's identity to be open, which
*-ever* supplies (Dayal 1997: *ever* quantifies over epistemic
alternatives; Heller & Wolter 2008: an *-ever* free relative "is
felicitous only when the speaker doesn't know, or doesn't care about, the
identity of the entity denoted"). Scoring backgroundedness on the same
frames is what tells them apart.

## Proposal: Negation and Discourse tasks on the same frames

Score the `-modifier` versions of Brett's frames (the first eight are
listed below; his protocol has sixteen, and any subset works), in both the
*whatever* and *what* versions, plus the April headed-relative and plain
free-relative frames, on Cuneo & Goldberg's Negation and Discourse tasks,
with participants separate from any rating study.

Outcomes, fixed before scoring:
- **O1:** *-ever* content scores as backgrounded as headed-relative
  content (equivalence test), yet the rating contrast holds. The
  backgrounding account fails for this cell.
- **O2:** *-ever* content scores markedly less backgrounded, and the
  rating contrast holds. Both accounts survive; this cell doesn't decide.
- **O3:** anything else, including the two tasks disagreeing.

Proposed thresholds, for discussion: O2 if the *-ever* minus headed
difference is at least half the headed-vs-main-clause gap with a 95%
interval excluding zero; O1 if the 90% interval lies within a quarter of
that gap. A go/no-go gate on raters, if used, should be stricter than six
of eight (chance passes it about 14% of the time).

The first eight frames:

1. The new release will still support {whatever / what} this feature is, but behind a setting.
2. He went straight to the minister with {whatever / what} it is that the analyst told him.
3. The introduction then eases into {whatever / what} the writer is trying to say.
4. Meaning can't be reduced to {whatever / what} is allowed in a physical description.
5. {Whatever / What} it was they were doing suddenly stopped.
6. The landlord blamed the leak on {whatever / what} the plumber had installed.
7. She gave the museum {whatever / what} her grandmother had left her.
8. The committee rejected {whatever / what} the consultant had proposed.

Frames 1-5 are adapted from attested web examples, 6-8 constructed.

## Optional, low priority: *the hell*

*The hell* in fused relatives is largely prior art. If there's room, a
few items on free-choice plain *what the hell* (*do what the hell you
like*, common in COCA) against referential plain *what the hell* (*do what
the hell I pay you for*, which Brett judges "somewhat dialectal"), with
the headed control (*the person who the hell called*).
