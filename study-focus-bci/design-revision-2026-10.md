## Why

Your February prediction, as recorded in the project notes, was that free
relatives would allow *exactly* where headed relatives don't. Part of it
looks right. In the frames searched in COCA, headed relatives with
*exactly*, *else* or *the hell* on the relative word don't occur, though
interrogative uses of the same frames do. But *-ever* free relatives take
post-head *exactly* (*X11 will still support whatever exactly this
feature is*, from Hacker News), and *whoever/whatever the hell* is common
in them, including edited text (CGEL p. 989 stars *Whoever the hell said
that was wrong*; Tredinnick 1995, p. 262, already had *-ever* free
relatives licensing *the hell*).

In five of the attested frames, my judgments (one judge, and I wrote the
squib) are that *exactly* costs a little after *whatever* and clearly more
after plain *what*: *The committee rejected whatever exactly the
consultant had proposed* vs *The committee rejected what exactly the
consultant had proposed*. I'm running that as a rating study (27 frames,
each rater 24, a pilot of about 30 and then 100-150 raters).

The rating study can show *that* the contrast exists, not *why*. Two
accounts make different predictions here:

- **BCI extended to these modifiers.** This is my extension, not BCI as
  published, which concerns long-distance dependencies. On it, *exactly*
  is degraded to the extent that the relative clause's content is
  backgrounded, so the *whatever* and *what* versions should differ in
  acceptability only if their content differs in backgroundedness.
- **An open-identity account.** *Exactly* needs the referent's identity to
  be open. An *-ever* free relative is a definite that presupposes its
  referent varies, across what the speaker knows (the ignorance reading)
  or across what might have been (the indifference reading). On the
  ignorance reading the speaker can't go on to name the referent
  (*whatever Arlo is cooking... \*namely, tomato soup*); on the
  indifference reading they can (*grabbed whatever was on the desk...
  namely, a stapler*) (Tredinnick 2005, pp. 2, 7-10). Heller & Wolter
  (2008) say an *-ever* free relative "is felicitous only when the
  speaker doesn't know, or doesn't care about, the identity of the
  entity denoted"; plain free relatives suggest this without requiring
  it. On this account *whatever exactly* needs the ignorance reading, and
  the contrast should hold even if the two versions are equally
  backgrounded.

Scoring backgroundedness on the same base sentences tells these apart.

## Proposal

Run the Negation and Discourse tasks on the base versions (no *exactly*)
of the rating study's frames, in both the *whatever* and the *what*
version, with participants separate from the rating study. Any subset of
the 27 frames can be used; the more frames, the tighter the comparison.

**Negation task** (as in Cuneo & Goldberg 2023, §2.1: participants rate on
a 5-point scale how far a negated base sentence still implies the content
of the target construction). Worked example for one frame:

- *The committee didn't reject whatever the consultant had proposed.* /
  *The committee didn't reject what the consultant had proposed.*
- "Does this sentence imply that the consultant had proposed something?"
  (1-5)

**Discourse task** (§2.2: participants choose which of two base sentences
is the "more direct and cooperative" response to a request for
information; the two differ only in whether the requested information sits
in the target construction). The response pairs for fused relatives need
designing together, since the requested information has to be the
relative clause's content in one option and a main clause in the other,
with propositional content matched as closely as possible.

**The comparison** is between the *whatever* and *what* versions, frame by
frame, on each task, alongside a few headed-relative and main-clause
anchors from the April items so the scale is interpretable.

**Possible outcomes**, to fix with you before any scoring:

- The *whatever* and *what* versions score about the same on
  backgroundedness, and the rating contrast holds. That counts against
  the BCI extension for this cell.
- The *whatever* versions score clearly less backgrounded, and the rating
  contrast holds. Both accounts survive; this cell doesn't decide.
- The two tasks disagree, or the measures are too noisy to say.

What counts as "about the same" and "clearly less" should be set from
pilot variance on these tasks; I'd rather agree that with you than
propose numbers.

## Optional, low priority: *the hell*

Free-choice plain *what the hell* (*do what the hell you like*) is common
in COCA; referential plain uses (*do what the hell I pay you for*) are
marginal on my reading. A few items on each, with a headed control, could
go in if there's room.

## Note on the January design

`design-expanded.md` (January) describes the Negation task as testing
whether negation denies the NP referent's existence, and the Discourse
task as asking which construction would come first. The descriptions
above follow Cuneo & Goldberg 2023 §§2.1-2.2 and supersede those.
