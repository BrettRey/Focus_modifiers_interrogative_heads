- **The definite-*what else* route to an *else* certificate is withdrawn.** Relayed from the Claude audit, it assumed a definite fused *what* can't host *else* (\**the thing else*). Lane C showed it fails: CGEL calls fused-relative NPs definite, "comparable to *We bought the tickets that were available*" (p. 398, `00-CGEL.md` l. 12123); a definite plural or non-count NP ordinarily reads as maximal (our step, not CGEL's wording); and *all* and the compound determinatives take *else* (p. 423, l. 12840), so the definite rows (E13-E15, COCA Round 2) paraphrase as *everything else you said...*. G's *else* clause can't be certified by attestation in any fused relative; headed *N who else* is the only route left (0 so far).
- **COCA Round 2 (Brett, by hand).** Definite plain fused × *the hell* could be looked for only in *do* frames: *eat, take, wear, buy, keep what the hell* returned 0 in every reading, free choice included. The 33 *do what the hell* tokens go to a blind Codex classification (free choice / indeterminate / referential, CGEL terms) before any status is assigned.
- **Prior art: Tredinnick 1994 via Dayal 1997.** Dayal (SALT 7, pp. 102, 106), reporting Tredinnick 1994, gives *I'll do what\*(ever) the hell detectives do*: *-ever* free relatives license *the hell*, plain ones don't, including on identity readings. The squib must cite this; CGEL p. 989 contradicts it. Dayal 1997 and Heller & Wolter 2008 (SALT 18: *-ever* FRs need speaker ignorance or indifference about identity) give outside motivation for the identity-open half of Lane A's narrowed account. Both added to `literature/`; notes in `orchestration/notes/reading-2026-10-27-the-hell-literature.md`.
- **Corpus awareness: *fusion-of-functions-revisited* §5 (`drafts/fused-relatives.tex`): deferred.** That paper argues for a second function in fused relatives from form that varies with function. This run's modifier facts (fused relative words take *the hell*; headed relative words don't) are a candidate further property, but the head analysis may account for them too, and the *else* attachment problem is the same head-vs-fusion question. Not adjudicated here.
- **Binding test for *else* in fused relatives: doesn't separate.** Constructed fused/headed pairs with a binder inside the relative clause (`orchestration/lanes/else-binding-pairs.md`). Brett: with *each*, the co-varying reading ("other than the one that guest named") is good in fused and headed versions alike, object and subject gaps alike; with *every*, neither version has it on a normal reading (the headed control allows a contrived universal-coverage story, the fused one doesn't). So the antecedent part can be read under the binder too, and *else* attachment in fused relatives remains undiagnosed. G's *else* clause stays untestable there. One judge.
- **Novelty check on *-ever* + *the hell* (den Dikken & Giannakidou 2002, Ippolito 2024, Martin 2020 read).** None discusses *the hell* in relatives, but the configuration already appears as unremarked acceptable data: *Sam believes that they'll catch whoever the hell it was* (Martin 2020, SALT 30, p. 342, (26b); repeated as Ippolito 2024, (61b)). Ippolito's entry for *the hell* requires an interrogative (Q[+int]) wh word; that example isn't a clean counterexample, since if *-ever* supplies a question (the run's own account of *whatever exactly*) it can satisfy that requirement. The cases bearing on her entry are plain fused *what the hell* (free choice, common; referential, judged dialectal). Corrected 2026-10-28 after a side agent's flag. The squib's finding is to be framed as systematic attestation plus the CGEL p. 989 conflict, not as a new observation. SPEC v2's "domain widening" gloss for *the hell* is contested (Ippolito: no at-issue contribution, doxastic dissonance; Martin: domain restriction); neutral wording if the spec is revised. Notes: `orchestration/notes/reading-2026-10-27-the-hell-literature.md`; Martin 2020 added to `literature/`.
